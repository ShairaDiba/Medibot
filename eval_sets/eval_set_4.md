2) Affects the cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, renal, and infectious body systems.
3) Classified as a critical condition that can occur in all age groups.
4) Common causes include drainage of an abscess (e.g., abscessed tooth), urinary tract infection with a bladder catheter, decubitus ulcers, intravenous procedures using unsterilized needles or prolonged IV placement, and the use of ostomy tubes such as gastrostomy, jejunostomy, or colostomy.

8) Prognosis is good when detected and treated early; however, untreated cases can progress to sepsis or septic shock, which can be fatal, especially in immunocompromised individuals.
9) Prevention focuses on personal hygiene, early treatment of bacterial infections, and careful management of invasive medical procedures.

## Question 5 - DEF-014 (Definition)

**Query given to MediBot:** What is allergic rhinitis?

**MediBot's answer:**

1) Definition: Allergic rhinitis, also called hay fever, is an inflammation of the nasal passages caused by an allergic reaction to airborne substances such as dander, dust, or pollen.
2) Types: There are two types - seasonal (occurs in spring, summer, early fall when pollen is high) and perennial (occurs year-round, often due to indoor allergens).
3) Causes: It is a type of immune reaction where the immune system responds to foreign allergens.
4) Symptoms: Inflammation of the nose leads to itching, sneezing, runny nose, redness, tenderness, sinus swelling that can cause ear congestion and popping, and mucus dripping down the throat.
5) Diagnosis: Seasonal allergic rhinitis is usually easy to diagnose and can often be done without a medical specialist.
6) Treatment: Options include decongestants (may cause side effects such as increased heart rate, insomnia, etc.) and topical corticosteroids to reduce mucous membrane inflammation; a combination of preventive strategies and medication is common.
7) Prognosis: Most people achieve adequate relief with preventive measures and treatment; allergies may improve, worsen, or expand over time, and early treatment can help prevent increased sensitization.
8) Prevention: Reduce pollen exposure by staying indoors with windows closed during high-pollen mornings, keeping car windows up, wearing a surgical mask outdoors, avoiding uncut fields, and learning which plants trigger symptoms.
9) Additional facts: Affects the immunological body system, moderate severity, can affect all ages, and is not contagious.

This is educational information, not a diagnosis.
